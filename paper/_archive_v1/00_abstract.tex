\begin{abstract}
Kirubakaran et al.\ propose governing cloud data pipelines with four policy-gated LLM agents and
report large reductions in recovery time, cost and manual intervention against static orchestration.
We replicate and extend that evaluation with a live remote language model rather than a simulation,
across a full matrix of eight configurations (three non-agent comparators, the full agent system, and
four single-agent ablations) over four injected fault scenarios ($\NRunsTotal{}$ runs total, three
independently resumable arms with per-run provenance). Against the static baseline, the full system
significantly reduces recovery time by $\NRelpctMttrSFull{}$\% (\NMttrFullMedian{}\,s vs.\
\NMttrBaselineMedian{}\,s median) and cost by $\NRelpctCostUnitsFull{}$\%, and executes the
accepted mitigation in a rate equivalent to Cliff's $\delta=\NDeltaDecisionCorrectFull{}$ of runs ---
but it also \emph{increases} manual interventions relative to the baseline, and two much cheaper,
non-LLM comparators (a rule-based responder and threshold autoscaling) reduce recovery time by
$\NRelpctMttrSRuleBased{}$\% and $\NRelpctMttrSAutoscale{}$\% respectively, beating every agent
configuration on speed. Run identically under a deterministic mock instead of the live model, the
same configuration's recovery time collapses to \NLiveVsMockMttrSMock{}\,s and its decision-correct
rate to \NLiveVsMockDecisionCorrectMock{} --- the evaluation path changes the answer by roughly three
orders of magnitude, which is the central methodological finding of this paper: governance claims
about these systems hold only for the evaluation path they were measured on. We further contribute a
generated adversarial corpus ($\NAdversarialCases{}$ cases) graded against an independently written
specification oracle, finding perfect containment ($\geq\NAdversarialWilsonLo{}$, Wilson 95\%) only
after fixing two contract-layer gaps the corpus itself found; closed-form sensitivity analyses for the
two modelled assumptions (simulated human latency, cost-model provisioning) that the headline numbers
depend on; and seven production-hardening lessons, each tied to a defect this system exhibited and
fixed. The architecture is not ours; the evidence, the measurement discipline, and the defects found
by holding the system to its own claims are.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} LLM agents; cloud data pipeline governance; policy-as-code; Open Policy
Agent; AIOps; replication study; adversarial evaluation; mock-vs-live evaluation validity.

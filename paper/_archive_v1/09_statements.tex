\section*{Ethics and Broader Impact}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Ethics and Broader Impact}

This study involves no human subjects, no personal data, and no data collected from or about real
people: all pipeline data is synthetic or seeded from public schemas
(Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}; \texttt{DATA\_\allowbreak LICENSES.md}), and the ``incidents''
evaluated are injected faults on a research testbed, not real production traffic. No IRB review
applies.

The broader-impact question this paper is actually positioned to speak to is narrower and more
concrete: what happens when an LLM agent is given the ability to act on infrastructure, gated by
policy rather than trusted outright. Two results in this paper bear directly on that question and
should not be separated from any summary of its contributions. First, the adversarial evaluation
(\S\ref{sec:results}) found the policy gate itself was not safe by default -- two real contract-layer
gaps (\S\ref{sec:lessons}, L6) had to be found and fixed before containment held at the reported rate --
so a system's stated safety guarantee is not evidence that guarantee holds until it has been
adversarially tested against an oracle independent of the code under test. Second, the live
decision-quality and manual-intervention results (\S\ref{sec:results}, \S\ref{sec:limitations})
together argue against deploying this class of system in a fully autonomous mode against real
infrastructure at the model and settings evaluated here; we report this as a finding, not a hedge, and
it is the basis for treating a graduated trust ladder (shadow, approval, autonomous, with a kill switch)
as the safe default rather than a conservative one (\S\ref{sec:discussion}, ``Ethics and autonomy'').
We are not aware of a dual-use path specific to this work beyond the general dual-use character of any
published agentic-automation research, which the containment and trust-ladder results above are
intended to inform rather than foreclose.

absence of specification is logged, with alternatives and rationale, in the repository's
design-deviation log (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}), so a reader can audit which choices were made
and why rather than take the system's behaviour on faith.

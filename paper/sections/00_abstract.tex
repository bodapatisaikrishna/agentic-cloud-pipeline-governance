\begin{abstract}
A recent proposal governs cloud data pipelines with four policy-gated LLM agents and reports
substantial gains over static orchestration. The original study does not disclose sufficient
implementation detail to determine the exact model-in-the-loop evaluation condition behind those
gains. We implement the system and evaluate it with a real, remote language model in a
\NRunsTotal{}-run campaign that crosses four injected fault scenarios with eight configurations: three
non-agent comparators, the full system, and four single-agent ablations. The runs are divided into
live-agent, non-agent, and mock-model arms. With a live model, the full system changes mean time to
recovery (MTTR) by \NRelpctMttrSFull{}\% and cost by \NRelpctCostUnitsFull{}\% relative to static
orchestration, with most of the cost change coming from an assumed provisioning gap. Yet it
\emph{increases} manual interventions by \NRelpctManualInterventionsFull{}\%. Two idealized non-LLM
baselines, each given a fixed modelled remediation latency, recover faster still, changing MTTR by
\NRelpctMttrSRuleBased{}\% and \NRelpctMttrSAutoscale{}\%. When the same \texttt{full} configuration
runs under a deterministic mock instead of the live model, recovery time falls to
\NLiveVsMockMttrSMock{}\,s, about three orders of magnitude below the live figure, and the
correct-mitigation score rises from \NDeltaDecisionCorrectFull{} to \NLiveVsMockDecisionCorrectMock{}.
A mock evaluation and a live evaluation of the same system therefore support different conclusions.
Graded against an oracle independent of the policy code, a generated adversarial corpus of
$\NAdversarialCases{}$ cases yields containment $\geq\NAdversarialWilsonLo{}$, but only after two
contract-layer gaps exposed by the corpus were fixed. The evidence is bounded by a single host,
synthetic data with injected faults, a simulated on-call engineer, one live model family, and
baselines that are modelled rather than deployed.
\end{abstract}

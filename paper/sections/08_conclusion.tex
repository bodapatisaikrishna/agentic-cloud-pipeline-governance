\section{CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK}\label{sec:conclusion}

We implemented and evaluated a policy-gated multi-agent design for cloud pipeline governance using a
live remote language model, explicit non-agent baselines, an adversarial evaluation against an
independent oracle, and closed-form sensitivity analyses. The design holds up only in part. Compared
with static orchestration, it shortens recovery time, though by less than the original report
claimed, and most of that reduction turns out to come from the simulated engineer rather than the
agents. It lowers cost by more than claimed, but nearly all of that saving comes from an assumed
provisioning gap. Manual interventions go up, and two idealized non-LLM comparators recover faster
still. The result we consider most important concerns the evaluation, not the design. With the same
code path and the same configuration, a deterministic mock and a live model lead to very different
conclusions. Recovery times differ by about three orders of magnitude, and the mock reports a perfect
correct-mitigation score that the live model comes nowhere near. For systems like this one, knowing
\emph{which} evaluation path produced a number matters at least as much as the number. We keep the
practical conclusion narrow on purpose. It says nothing about LLM agents in operations in general.
It does say that a governance claim should come with an account of how it was evaluated, and that
mock and live numbers are separate findings until both have been measured.

We see two directions for future work. One is to collect the evidence this campaign lacks: a live
cross-model sweep to test model independence, a test of the trust core's mode transitions and
kill-switch latency under load, a pre-registered re-run with the corrected simulator, per-run state
resets and a fixed cost window, and deployed controllers and real production traces in place of the two modelled baselines
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). The other is design work: replacing the
static agent-priority bid with an explicit negotiation protocol, enabling the bounded-adaptation
mechanism that is currently disabled to preserve determinism, and extending the evaluation to
multi-cloud deployments and to formal verification of the policy layer.

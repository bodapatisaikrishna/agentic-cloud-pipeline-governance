\section{CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper reported an implementation and evaluation of a policy-gated multi-agent design for
governing cloud pipelines, using a live remote language model, explicit non-agent baselines, an
adversarial corpus graded by an independent oracle and closed-form sensitivity analyses. The design is
only partly supported by the evidence. Against static orchestration it shortens recovery, though less
than the original report claimed, and most of that reduction turned out to come from the simulated
engineer rather than from the agents; it lowers cost by more than claimed, but almost entirely through
an assumed provisioning gap; and it increases manual interventions, while two idealized non-LLM
comparators recover faster still. In our judgement the most important result is about the
evaluation rather than the design. Run along the same code path with the same configuration, a
deterministic mock and a live model led to quite different conclusions, with recovery times about
three orders of magnitude apart and a perfect correct-mitigation score under the mock that the live
model did not come close to. For systems of this kind, then, the evaluation path behind a number
matters at least as much as the number itself. We have deliberately kept the practical conclusion
narrow and draw nothing from it about LLM agents in operations generally; what it does imply is that a
governance claim should be accompanied by an account of how it was evaluated, and that mock and live
numbers should be treated as separate findings until both have been measured.

Future work falls into two parts. One is to collect the evidence this campaign lacks: a live
cross-model sweep to test model independence, a test of the trust core's mode transitions and
kill-switch latency under load, a pre-registered re-run with the corrected simulator, per-run state
resets and a fixed cost window, and deployed controllers and real production traces in place of the
two modelled baselines
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). The other is design work: replacing the
static agent-priority bid with an explicit negotiation protocol, enabling the bounded-adaptation
mechanism that is currently disabled to preserve determinism, and extending the evaluation to
multi-cloud deployments and to formal verification of the policy layer.
